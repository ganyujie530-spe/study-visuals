Strong induction: a polygon with n sides splits into n − 2 triangles

BSC129 Discrete Mathematics · Topic 4 Induction, Week 8 slide 17. Example: a hexagon, n = k + 1 = 6. Use the buttons or ← → keys.

P(n): a simple n-gon splits into n-2 triangles
Basis: P(3): a triangle is 1 = 3-2 triangle.
Assume P(3), P(4), ..., P(k) all true, k ≥ 3.
Take a (k+1)-gon. It has an interior diagonal.
The diagonal cuts it into an s-gon and a t-gon,
  s + t = (k+1) + 2,  so 3 ≤ s, t ≤ k.
By the hypothesis: s-2 and t-2 triangles.
Total: (s-2)+(t-2) = s+t-4 = k-1 = (k+1)-2.
By strong induction, P(n) for all n ≥ 3.

The key count, s + t = n + 2: the diagonal is a side of both pieces, so it is counted twice.

Why strong induction: the pieces can have any number of sides from 3 to k (a hexagon gives 4 + 4 or 3 + 5), not only k.

Show the pieces are in range: each piece has at least 3 sides, so the other has at most k; write 3 ≤ s, t ≤ k.

Quoted fact: every simple polygon with at least 4 sides has an interior diagonal. Use it, do not prove it.
