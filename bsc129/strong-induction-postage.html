Strong induction: 4-cent and 5-cent stamps make every amount from 12

BSC129 Discrete Mathematics · Topic 4 Induction, Week 8 slide 16. Four columns, each one goes down by +4. Use the buttons or ← → keys.

P(n): n cents can be made from 4c and 5c stamps
Basis: 12 = 4+4+4,  13 = 4+4+5,
       14 = 4+5+5,  15 = 5+5+5
Assume P(12), ..., P(k) all true, some k ≥ 15.
Since k ≥ 15:  12 ≤ k-3 ≤ k,
so P(k-3) is true (inductive hypothesis).
Add one 4-cent stamp: (k-3) + 4 = k+1.
So P(k+1) is true.
By strong induction, P(n) for all n ≥ 12.

Four columns because the step is +4: each amount is the one 4 above it plus one 4-cent stamp. Only the top of each column must be made by hand.

Basis = the top row: 12, 13, 14, 15 are checked one by one; they have nothing 4 above them inside the range.

Why k ≥ 15: it guarantees k-3 ≥ 12, so the cell 4 above is inside the range the hypothesis covers. Without it the proof has a gap.

Strong, not ordinary: P(k+1) uses P(k-3), not P(k), so the hypothesis must cover all of P(12) ... P(k).
