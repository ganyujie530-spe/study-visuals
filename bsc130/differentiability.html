Differentiable: the tangent plane's error shrinks faster than the step

BSC130 Calculus II B · Chapter 14.4 Tangent Planes and Linear Approximations (slides p.28–32). Example f(x, y) = xexy at (1, 0). Use the buttons or ← → keys; drag the sliders from step 4.

distance = √(Δx² + Δy²), how far the point moved from (1, 0).

f(x, y) = xexy,   (a, b) = (1, 0),   f(1, 0) = 1
fx = exy + xyexy,     fy = x²exy
fx(1, 0) = 1,     fy(1, 0) = 1
L(x, y) = f(a, b) + fx(a, b)(x − a) + fy(a, b)(y − b)
           = 1 + (x − 1) + y = x + y
Δz = f(a + Δx, b + Δy) − f(a, b)     (surface)
dz = fx(a, b) Δx + fy(a, b) Δy     (plane)
Δz = dz + ε₁Δx + ε₂Δy,   ε₁, ε₂ → 0 as (Δx, Δy) → (0, 0)
Theorem: fx, fy exist near (a, b) and are
   continuous at (a, b)  ⇒  f is differentiable at (a, b)

What "differentiable" means: Δz = dz + ε₁Δx + ε₂Δy with ε₁, ε₂ → 0. The error is (small) × (small), so error ÷ distance → 0: the tangent plane fits the surface well near the point.

Linearization = tangent plane: L(x, y) = f(a,b) + fx(a, b)(x − a) + fy(a, b)(y − b). Near (a, b), f ≈ L. Here xexy ≈ x + y near (1, 0).

To show differentiable: find fx and fy, say they exist and are continuous at the point, then quote the theorem. A sufficient condition only: the reverse direction is not guaranteed.

Two variables ≠ one variable: partial derivatives existing alone do NOT give differentiability. You need the extra condition (e.g. continuity of fx, fy).
